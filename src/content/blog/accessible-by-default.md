Accessibility is often treated as a final-week checklist item. That is exactly
why it so often fails. Built in from the start, it is inexpensive and invisible.
Bolted on at the end, it is costly and brittle.

## It is the right thing to do

A meaningful share of your audience uses assistive technology — screen readers,
keyboard navigation, high-contrast modes. An inaccessible site simply turns those
people away at the door.

## It is also good engineering

The same habits that make a site accessible — semantic HTML, labelled controls,
visible focus states, sufficient contrast — make it more robust, easier to test,
and friendlier to search engines, which read your markup much like a screen reader does.

## Make it the default

Reach for a real button before a clickable div. Give every image meaningful alt
text. Ensure every interactive element is reachable and operable by keyboard. Do
this by default and accessibility stops being a project — it becomes how you build.
